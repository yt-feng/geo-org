Eco-GEO: Supply-chain AI visibility from zero to one—check eligibility before comparing fact repair with coverage expansion

With fixed hours, a fixed question set and independent review, first check error rates and the net increase in qualifying citations, then compare the full costs of feasible options. In the illustrative m=20 case, E_base>0.10 excludes B. Holding other baseline inputs fixed, qA=0.60 gives A an E_A of 0.20, so choose C; qA≥0.80 gives E_A≤0.10 and ΔQ_A=8, so choose A. At m=2, reducing hB alone to≤1.625 hours or increasing pB alone to≥4/13 lets B reach the minimum increment. All numbers are illustrative; final acceptance uses new error counts, net increments and actual full hours.

Core judgment: exclude options that fail the thresholds before comparing fact repair with coverage expansion.

Condition: During a cold start period of 0-1, the channel manager has 80 hours and 8 weeks at his disposal (a indicative budget). Brand-related facts are scattered across old quotation sheets, expired certification pages, and various dealer statements. Additionally, competitors’ references can be found in the AI answers. The choices are: A) First unify the facts and fix expired and incorrect information; B) First conduct issue coverage and content expansion; C) Postpone expanding GEO. All three options share the same budget, observation window, and set of issues.

  • Thresholds come before ranking, but they do not automatically favor A. In the illustrative case m=20 (E_base=0.50), B is excluded because its baseline error rate exceeds 0.10. Holding the other baseline inputs fixed, qA=0.60 gives E_A=0.20, so A also fails and the decision is C. With qA≥0.80, E_A≤0.10 and ΔQ_A=8, making A the only feasible option. Equality holds only at qA=0.80; for example, qA=0.90 gives E_A=0.05.
  • The upper limit of the number of repaired objects is determined by both the number of errors and the unqualified spaces, not by man-hours alone. nA=min(floor(52/hA), m, Q_cap). High error rates increase the difficulty of meeting the A standard (more errors need to be eliminated to stay within the threshold), and they may also prevent available objects from being used by m.
  • At N=40, A and B cannot both qualify. If pA=0, A has zero increment and is excluded. For pA>0, A needs m≥8/pA to reach ΔQ≥8, while B requires m≤0.10N at baseline. Thus N≥80/pA is necessary for joint feasibility; N≥200 follows only when pA=0.40. All other conditions must still pass. The example therefore mainly asks whether to select the sole feasible option or pause.

Not applicable: For enterprises with no versioned fact ledger, no independent review personnel, unstable issue extraction (N<20), or insufficient qualified space (Q_cap<8), this calculation does not apply. These enterprises should directly proceed to step C and provide additional evidence first. All numerical values in the text are hypothetical and not industry benchmarks or actual measurement results for any enterprise.

1. Mechanism assumptions and falsification methods: Evidence only reaches “qualification and relevance”; it does not reach “effect”.

Google’s search documentation states that AI Overviews and AI Mode may use “query fan-out,” initiating multiple related searches across sub-topics and multiple data sources. This can result in a broader set of links compared to traditional searches. A page must be indexed and eligible to display summaries in searches to become a supported link, with no additional technical requirements. [S1] This document describes eligibility criteria and general practices, but does not promise any brand-specific growth in citations.

This suggests a hypothesis: if a brand parameter has conflicting versions across pages, different subqueries may retrieve different versions and produce inconsistent answers to the same intent. The proposed mediator is multiple public versions of one fact. The observable test is whether answers to the fixed question set repeat outdated or incorrect versions. The explanation fails if unifying all versions does not reduce incorrect answers. Propagation across answer paths is a mechanism to test, not an established engineering fact.

A v1 preprint on arXiv conducted extensive measurements: covering 55,936 queries, six large-model-based search systems, and two traditional search systems. It reported that 37% of the domain names in its sample appeared only in the large-model search side. The domain names favored by these systems were more structured in terms of features (hierarchical HTML), with easier-to-read text, lower domain popularity, and more external links pointing to high-reputation sources. [S3]These are feature associations, not causal interventions. It cannot be inferred that “more external links pointing to high-reputation sources will increase the probability of being cited.” The check direction it suggests is: if the bottleneck of this brand lies in page structure and readability, there is limited room for further investment in error correction.

The same preprint also cited two existing studies in the literature review: one study suggested that incorrect information in the retrieved documents might spread to the generated answers (Deng et al., 2025); another study argued that users would be influenced by the number and type of citations, even if the attribution was incorrect (Miroyan et al., 2025). [S3] are existing papers cited by S3, not experimental conclusions of S3 itself. This paper does not consider “the existence of citations” as evidence of effectiveness.

Microsoft’s Copilot Search announcement says the product highlights sources, links complete sentences or paragraphs to their sources, and lists all links used to generate the answer. It also states that availability excludes China and Russia. [S5] This is a vendor feature claim, not proof of citation accuracy or conversion effectiveness. Availability of the same intervention may differ across platforms for readers in China.

The OECD policy report distinguishes routine disruptions, addressed through ordinary enterprise management, from extreme events that call for government facilitation and emergency resources. Applying ordinary tools to major disruptions can be wasteful and ineffective. [S2] A public consulting commentary describes explicit trade-offs: concentrated sourcing can reduce complexity and cost, while diversification can improve resilience at greater cost and complexity. [S4] Neither source studies GEO. Their contribution is a methodological analogy—segment decisions by conditions and include all costs—not evidence of AI citation effectiveness.

2. Caliber: Problem sets, two rounds of baseline, and differences between N and Q_cap targets

For example, consider a fixed set of 40 questions (for illustration), with each question representing a sample unit. The questions are selected by channel managers from nearly 12 months of inquiry and sales interactions for this brand, after deduplication, and then frozen in terms of platform, version, language, question type, and sampling period. Operations staff complete two rounds of baseline sampling, and final rechecks are conducted using the same procedures. Each question retains its answer and source link. Independent reviewers determine the answers based on version-specific facts, with disagreements resolved by a third party. Different platforms report separately, without combining denominators.

“Qualified references” require traceable references in both rounds, and the brand-related facts involved in the question must be correct within the agreed verification range. Questions with incorrect brand facts in any round are counted as errors and are not excluded based on severity—severity grading requires separate criteria, which are not used in this article. Error questions do not overlap with qualified questions. The number of baseline error questions is denoted as m, E_base = m/N; Q_base is the integer count of qualified questions (illustrated as 6), Q_cap = N - Q_base = 34, representing the remaining unqualified space. Here, the two quantities must be separated: N is the total number of questions in the pool, with a threshold of 20 questions; Q_cap is the potential improvement space in this round, with a threshold of 8 questions. If either condition is not met, choose C directly for (0), the action is the same, with no difference in order.

  • E: Numerator = number of questions containing brand-related factual errors; Denominator = N = 40; Sampling unit = question; Collector = channel operation; Window = two consecutive rounds before intervention; Deviation control = same collector, same question format, same time period; The question set is not modified between the two rounds.
  • Q is the integer count of qualifying questions. The qualifying proportion is Q/N; a proportion must not be confused with a count. An independent third-party reviewer checks the start and end of the same observation window. Retain both answers and sources. The disagreement rate is the number of disputed questions divided by the number reviewed.
  • ΔQ=Q_end−Q_base is the end-minus-start change in the count of qualifying questions within the same question set and observation window, measured in questions. The change in qualifying proportion is ΔQ/N. Do not substitute the end-of-period total for the increment.

B’s eligible pool is U=Q_cap−m only if every question has undergone fact verification, every in-scope brand factual error is included in m, and erroneous questions do not overlap with qualifying questions. If these conditions fail, do not use the formula; establish the evidence first. This article promises consistent definitions and reviewable calculations, not statistical significance. No significance markers were retained in the read excerpt, so it cannot establish whether any effect is statistically significant.

3. Thresholds and sorting rules: First, eliminate those that do not meet the criteria. Then, compare them. Finally, sort them accordingly.

The pre-frozen indicative threshold (calibration method: first determine the stable level that this brand can achieve through two rounds of baseline testing, then review the results. The threshold must not be changed afterward): N≥20, Q_cap≥8, total planned hours for the project ≤80 hours, E_post≤0.10, ΔQ≥8, review disagreement rate <20%. 0.10, 8 questions, 20 questions are all test values to be calibrated, not industry parameters given by the source.

Apply the decision rules in order. (0) Choose C if the data conditions fail: N<20, Q_cap<8, no versioned fact ledger, no independent review, any unverified in-scope brand fact, or a disagreement rate≥20%. (1) Exclude any option with total hours>80 or expected ΔQ≤0 without calculating its unit cost. A requires ΔQ_A≥8 and expected E_A≤0.10. B requires E_base≤0.10, expected E_B≤0.10 and ΔQ_B≥8. The forecast E_B=m/N holds only under the illustrative assumption that B introduces and removes no errors; actual acceptance must recalculate E_post. (2) Select the sole feasible option; if neither is feasible, choose C. (3) If both are feasible, compare full unit costs H/ΔQ and prefer the lower cost. If costs tie, prefer the higher increment. If both costs and increments tie, collect further observations before deciding.

Keep two uses of equality separate. Equality at an eligibility threshold: E_post=0.10, ΔQ=8, N=20, Q_cap=8 and total hours=80 all pass. The disagreement rate must be<20%; a rate≥20% means C, consistent with the fixed criteria and step (0). Equality in ranking: when unit costs tie, choose the higher ΔQ; when both tie, gather more observations before deciding. These are different rules, not interchangeable uses of equality.

Step (3) cannot occur in this example: at N=40, B requires m≤4, while A has ΔQ_A≤m×pA≤m≤4<8. In general, exclude pA=0 first because A cannot meet the minimum increment. For pA>0, A requires m≥8/pA and B requires m≤0.10N, giving the necessary condition N≥80/pA. Only fixing pA=0.40 gives N≥200. This is not sufficient: eligible counts, hours, error rates and B’s increment must also pass.

Consider A’s feasible range with illustrative inputs pA=0.40, hA=1.6, N=40 and Q_cap=34. Available hours cap nA at floor(52/1.6)=32. For m≤32, nA=m, and ΔQ_A≥8 requires m≥20. If qA=0.80, E_A=m(1−0.80)/40≤0.10 requires m≤20, so the only feasible integer m is 20. For m>32, nA=32 and E_A=(m−25.6)/40 still fails. If qA=0.90, integers m=20 through 32 pass; for m>32, E_A=(m−28.8)/40≤0.10 requires m≤32.8, so integers 33 and 34 fail. A higher error rate therefore does not automatically favor repair: too few erroneous questions cannot meet the increment threshold, while too many cannot meet the residual-error threshold.

Write the rules as executable statements, corresponding to (0)(1)(2)(3) item by item, without any additional prerequisites:

  • Step 0: If N<20, Q_cap<8, there is no versioned fact ledger or independent review, any in-scope brand fact remains unverified, or the review disagreement rate is≥20%, choose C and end the procedure.
  • Step 1: First exclude options with total hours>80 or expected ΔQ≤0. For the rest, A is feasible if and only if ΔQ_A≥8 and expected E_A≤0.10; B is feasible if and only if E_base≤0.10, ΔQ_B≥8 and expected E_B≤0.10. Actual acceptance always uses a newly sampled E_post, not the baseline or forecast.
  • Step 2: Neither A nor B feasible → C; only A feasible → A; only B feasible → B. B may fail because E_base>0.10, or because its error-rate or increment condition fails. A may fail its error-rate or increment condition. Do not add B’s baseline condition as a prerequisite for selecting the only feasible option A.
  • Step 3: If both options are feasible, prefer the lower full unit cost H/ΔQ. If costs tie, prefer the higher ΔQ. If both cost and ΔQ tie, gather further observations before deciding.

For the m=20 rows qA, pA, hA and S in Table 2, E_base=0.50 excludes B at step 1. A is the sole feasible option only in columns that pass all of that row’s conditions; a column in which A fails means C. Selecting the sole feasible A at step 2 does not require B’s E_base≤0.10 condition.

4. Simplified ledger: How 80 hours can be closed in two scenarios

Shared preliminary work is required for either A or B and is included in each option’s full cost: 10 hours for the fact ledger +12 hours for the two baseline rounds and final sampling +6 hours for independent review and adjudication =28 hours, leaving 52 intervention hours. This shared work organizes evidence and measurement without changing public pages; the model assigns it no citation increment. A and B are alternative trials, not two separate allocations of 80 hours.

A: hA=1.6 hours per question, illustratively split into 0.8 for checking and 0.8 for rewriting and submission; nA=min(floor((80−S)/hA),m,Q_cap); ΔQ_A=nA×pA; E_A=(m−nA×qA)/N; H_A=S+hA×nA; 0≤pA≤qA≤1. B handles only questions whose facts are verified as correct but which do not yet have qualifying citations; hB=2.0 hours per question; U=Q_cap−m; nB=min(floor((80−S)/hB),U); ΔQ_B=nB×pB; H_B=S+hB×nB; 0≤pB≤1. Baseline shared work S=28 leaves 52 intervention hours; if S changes, recompute 80−S. Require 0≤S≤80 and positive hours per question. The illustrative forecast E_B=m/N assumes B neither removes existing errors nor adds new ones. At the end, calculate E_post from the new error count. For example, starting at m=2, one new erroneous question with none removed gives E_post=3/40=0.075, which still passes≤0.10; three new erroneous questions give 5/40=0.125 and fail. New errors do not automatically mean exceeding 10%, and the old baseline cannot establish a pass. If B must first complete A, include the repair hours and reset the shared baseline; do not use this independent-B ledger.

High error scenario (example): m=20, Q_cap=34, pA=0.40, qA=0.60, hA=1.6 → nA=min(32,20,34)=20; ΔQ_A=8; Q_end=6+8=14; E_A=(20−12)/40=0.20; H_A=28+32=60. Budget closure: 10+12+6+32=60. B is excluded in step 1 due to E_base=0.50; ΔQ_B and unit cost are not calculated. Conclusion C: A also fails because its error rate exceeds the allowed maximum. If qA is calibrated to 0.80, then E_A=(20−16)/40=0.10, ΔQ_A=8, and A becomes the only viable option; the action is set to A.

Low error scenario (example): m=2, U=32, hB=2.0, pB=0.25, pA=0.40, qA=0.60 → A: nA=min(32,2,34)=2, ΔQ_A=0.8, E_A=(2−1.2)/40=0.02 (meets error threshold but increment is insufficient), H_A=28+3.2=31.2, unit cost = 31.2/0.8=39; B: nB=min(26,32)=26, ΔQ_B=6.5, Q_end=12.5, E_B=0.05 (hypothetical, requires re-measurement), H_B=28+52=80, unit cost = 80/6.5≈12.31. Both ΔQ<8 are not feasible → C. The values 39 and 12.31 here are for completeness only; the threshold has excluded both options, so cost comparison no longer affects the decision.

Integer execution: nA and nB are integer counts of interventions. ΔQ is a model expectation and may be fractional; it is not an observed count. Final acceptance uses actual integer counts of erroneous and qualifying questions among the 40. Q_end must exceed the baseline by at least 8 (Q_base=6 → Q_end≥14). If previously qualifying questions are lost, subtract those losses: measure the net increment, not only newly qualifying questions.

The remaining working hours are accounted for using conditional rules: if a plan uses less than 52 hours of intervention capacity (for example, nA=20 uses only 32 hours), the remaining working hours are filled in according to pre-established rules by filling in missing fields in the fact ledger (each field is assumed to be 1.0 hours). If there are no missing fields to fill, they are recorded as unused and reassigned in the next budget cycle. It is not allowed to calculate costs based on all 52 hours while ignoring the remaining capacity. The budget closure sentence in Section 7 follows the same criteria as this section.

In other words, there are only two scenarios that truly change A/B/C: when the conversion rate exceeds limits (qA, pA, pB rows) causing C to become A or B; when the capacity exceeds limits (hA, hB, S rows) causing A or B to become C.

The algebraic intersection of the two expected increments in the low-error example is pB=0.8/26=2/65≈0.030769. For pB=0.02, 2/65 and 0.05, ΔQ_B is respectively 0.52, 0.8 and 1.3. All are below 8, so all three cases choose C. Use the exact fraction for equality; the rounded 0.0308 is not the exact intersection. The intersection is below the illustrative range 0.15–0.55 and has no actionable significance. The actionable low-error threshold is pB≥4/13, holding nB=26 and hB=2.0 fixed. The unit costs of 39 and 12.31 hours per additional qualifying question are shown only for completeness: both options already fail the increment threshold, so comparing costs does not change the decision.

In this N=40 example, changing the inputs listed in the table cannot make both options feasible while B still requires m≤4 and A repairs only erroneous questions with pA≤1. Both cannot reach ΔQ≥8, so there is no ranking of jointly feasible A and B. In general, pA=0 excludes A. For pA>0, a necessary condition for joint feasibility is N≥80/pA; only fixing pA=0.40 yields N≥200. This is not sufficient: check hours, error rates, eligible object counts and B’s increment separately. N≥200 alone does not establish that both options are feasible.

6. The most threatening opposing interpretations and invalidation conditions

An alternative explanation is that the brand’s citation shortfall comes mainly from readability and structure rather than factual errors. S3 includes structured HTML and readable text among its observed features, [S3] so this explanation is compatible with the cited evidence. For every nonqualifying question, record possible causes—missing facts, incorrect facts, structure/readability or lack of third-party evidence—allowing multiple causes, and report their shares across the two baseline rounds. This diagnosis adds no threshold and does not override steps (0)–(3). If A’s pilot still gives ΔQ_A<8, step (1) rejects A regardless of the labels assigned to causes. The labels guide the next pilot toward B or structural changes; they do not waive eligibility. If errors decline but net citation growth remains 0, the proposed correction-to-growth mechanism fails and expansion stops. A predominance of factual causes offers partial support for A’s mechanism in this brand’s setting, but the decision still follows (0)–(3), without a separate diagnostic cutoff.

Opposition Explanation 2: The real bottleneck is that the problem pool is too small (N<20 or Q_cap<8). In this case, neither A nor B is reliable.
